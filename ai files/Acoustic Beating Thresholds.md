# Acoustic Beating Thresholds Near C4

## How Beating Works

When two tones are close in frequency, you hear amplitude modulation (beats) at the **difference frequency** (f₂ − f₁). As the interval widens, the beat rate increases until it transitions into perceived "roughness" and eventually into two distinct pitches.

## Thresholds

### Clear, Countable Beats (~0–15 Hz difference)
At C4 (262 Hz), this corresponds to roughly **0–1 semitone** apart. A semitone up (C#4 ≈ 277 Hz) gives ~15 Hz beating — fast but still perceptible as beats/flutter. A quarter tone above C4 (~269 Hz) gives only ~7 Hz beating — very clear, slow beats.

### Roughness Zone (~15–40 Hz difference)
The beats become too fast to count individually and merge into a "rough" or "buzzy" quality. This corresponds to roughly **1–2.5 semitones** above C4 (~277–300 Hz).

### Beating Becomes Inaudible / Smooth
Beyond roughly **30–40 Hz difference** (around **2–3 semitones** at this register), the two tones start to sound like a smooth interval rather than a beating/rough pair. This is approximately a **minor third** or slightly less.

## Key Landmarks Near C4

| Interval               | Frequency | Difference | Perception                    |
|------------------------|-----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| Quarter tone (~50¢)    | ~269 Hz   | ~7 Hz      | Slow, very clear beats        |
| Semitone (100¢)        | ~277 Hz   | ~15 Hz     | Fast beating / flutter        |
| Three-quarter tone (~150¢) | ~285 Hz | ~23 Hz   | Roughness, beating fading     |
| Whole tone (200¢)      | ~294 Hz   | ~32 Hz     | Rough but less beating        |
| Minor third (300¢)     | ~311 Hz   | ~49 Hz     | Mostly smooth, beating gone   |

## The Critical Bandwidth (Plomp & Levelt, 1965)

Two tones within the same **critical band** produce roughness/beating. At C4, the critical bandwidth is approximately **3 semitones** (roughly a minor third).

Maximum roughness occurs at about **25% of the critical bandwidth** — roughly **0.75–1 semitone** at C4.

## Summary by Interval Size (at C4)

- **Quarter tones and below:** Very clear, countable beating
- **Semitone to three-quarter tone:** Fast beating transitioning to roughness
- **Beyond ~2 semitones:** Roughness fading
- **Beyond ~3 semitones (minor third):** Beating essentially gone

## Register Dependence

The critical bandwidth **widens in lower registers**:
- **Cello C3 (~131 Hz):** Beating persists over a wider interval (up to ~4 semitones)
- **Violin registers:** Critical bandwidth narrows, so beating stops sooner

This means the same interval (e.g., a semitone) will produce more prominent beating on cello than on violin.
